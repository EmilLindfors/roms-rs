# Geometry and meshes for real coastlines (P1.3)

### P1.3 Geometry for real coastlines
- [x] Per-node isoparametric geometric factors (`[K]` → `[K × n_nodes]`), parallelogram-only panic removed (2026-09-26). `GeometricFactors2D::compute(mesh, ops)` evaluates the bilinear map at every node: metric, J, mass weights w·J, and per face node the normal and sJ from the same contravariant vectors (`sJ n = ±J∇r, ±J∇s`), so the discrete metric identities hold to round-off.
  - Kernels: the collocated SWE, tracer, advection and BR1 diffusion volume terms are in conservative form `J⁻¹[Dr(J∇r·F) + Ds(J∇s·F)]` with the per-node lift scale sJ/J; the split forms use the curvilinear Wintermeyer et al. (2017) form (`{{Ja}}` metric averages, bed term included); the `WetDry` subcells use the telescoping interface metrics (Fisher et al. 2013; Hennemann et al. 2021) plus a metric balance term in the bed source (zero on parallelograms and flat beds). Limiter, wet/dry and diagnostic means are mass-weighted (`LimiterContext2D` and the limiter / wet-dry functions take `&GeometricFactors2D`).
  - Gates on distorted periodic meshes (`tests/curvilinear_2d_test.rs`): free stream, lake at rest over rough and shoreline beds, mass/momentum/tracer conservation, entropy; convergence N+1 for advection and the split forms, 2 for the subcells (`convergence_test.rs`, docs/accuracy.md).
  - Parallelograms take a fast path from a dense per-element copy of the node-0 geometry (`ElementGeometry`): the per-node fields are strided and cost ≈ 9 cache lines per element. Frøya (all parallelograms, `profile=80`, mains power, CPUs 0–7, five alternating runs, min/median): RHS 1 thread 4.44/4.51 ms against 4.36/4.38 on `main` (+2–3 %), 24 threads 1.22/1.26 against 1.17/1.20 (+4–5 %); `dt` 0.246/0.251 against 0.259/0.262 (−5 %); step at 24 threads +4–5 %. Before the fast paths the RHS was ≈ 10–20 % slower. [ ] Find the remaining few percent with a profiler (AMD uProf is installed).
  - [x] The 3D kernels on general quadrilaterals (2026-10-03, `tests/curvilinear_3d_test.rs`). `Hydrostatic3D::new` no longer asserts parallelograms.
    - The PGF takes the metric per node and per face node, the 3D limiters weight with `w_i J_i`, and the 3D step takes each node's length (now the narrow side of elongated elements, as in 2D: 10:1 rectangles get 1.74× shorter steps).
    - The 3D divergences take the 2D module's metric form (`MetricForm`): the split forms' mass flux is `{{J∇r}}·{{hu}}`, not the collocated `D(J∇r·hu)`. Without the match the splitter's nodal identity failed everywhere, and a distorted seamount at rest jumped to 0.9 m/s. The PGF takes the averaged metric as the advection's adjoint.
    - Gates: rest, exactness, constancy, inventories, momentum, PGF and advection convergence (see CHANGELOG).
  - [ ] `batched` SIMD and the Burn prototype still use one metric per element (`affine_metric`); `compute_volume_terms_batched` and `BurnGeometricFactors2D::from_cpu` assert parallelograms.

          - Mechanism. The split form exchanges potential energy with the σ-pairs force exactly for any ρ, and it also conserves the tracer variance `Σ H_z ρ²`. Where a σ-level crosses the pycnocline between two nodes of one element, the pair's chord is far steeper than the stratification at either node.

        - **Literature and analysis 2026-10-04: `docs/stratified-rest-over-steep-beds.md`.** In short:
          - The literature knows the instability. Beckmann & Haidvogel 1993 found z-interpolated PGFs less stable, as the constant-depth form is here. Zängl 2012: unstable when neighbours' heights differ by much more than the layer spacing. Shepherd 1993: energy alone is not enough, rest needs a Casimir (the APE). Ricardo, Duru & Lee 2024 saw the same failure in thermal shallow water.
          - Operational answers are geometric: NorKyst smooths to rx0 0.34 with Haney ≈ 6 and keeps levels near the surface; SCHISM LSC² uses z-based gradients on per-node grids; multi-envelope levels follow smoothed virtual bottoms; ROMS wet–dry masks the flux and the PGF together.
          - With pairs by level index, conservation and exact energy, a stable rest forces the pair density's linearisation, so its stiffness (≈ chord/2G: up to 65 at the slice's cliff, half its levels > 10) is unavoidable. An entropy-*stable* pair (clipped toward upwind) equals `ρ*` at Q = 0, so it is stiff too, unless discontinuous in Q (Coulomb friction ≈ 3e-3 m/s² at the cliff).
          - Anomaly diffusion can feed the APE across a straddling pair (two nodes at G = 0.25 and 4e-4 kg/m⁴: `sym(G⁻¹L)` has an eigenvalue of +516). That explains why it only slowed the growth.
