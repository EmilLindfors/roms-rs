# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added

- **Reference potential energy: a measure of spurious mixing in 3D (TODO P4.5).** New `solver::PotentialEnergy3D` (re-exported from `solver`): the potential energy `g ∫ (ρ − ρ_r) z dV` of a `Solution3D` and its reference (background) potential energy, the same water sorted by density into level layers over the basin's own hypsometry (Winters et al. 1995). Each GLL node is a column of its quadrature area over its bed; each wet layer a parcel, stacked exactly over the piecewise-constant area. RPE is raised only by diapycnal mixing, so with no explicit diffusivity its growth is the advection's spurious mixing (Ilıcak et al. 2012). `PotentialEnergy::available()` is `total − reference`. Energies are of the anomaly from a reference density `ρ_r` (the model's ρ₀); changes of RPE do not depend on it, and full-density energies lose ≈ 1e-10 of a diffusion step's change to round-off.
  - Gates: a level stable stratification is its own sorted state (APE 1e-14 of PE); the lock's APE is `g A H² Δρ/8`; the reference basin follows a stepped bed; dry columns hold no water; under a constant vertical κ a stratified basin at rest gains exactly `g κ A Δt (ρ_bed − ρ_surface)` per implicit diffusion step, to 4.2e-11 (`vertical_diffusion_raises_the_reference_potential_energy_at_its_exact_rate`).
  - **The lock exchange's spurious mixing is set by the grid Reynolds number `ΔU·Δx/ν`**, as Ilıcak et al. found: at `Re_Δ` = 10 it is 5.1–5.7e-4 W/m² from 500 m to 62 m P2 elements (hours 1–3), and 3.818e-4 against 3.812e-4 on 250 and 125 m over hours 1–2 (gate `spurious_mixing_is_set_by_the_grid_reynolds_number`). Without viscosity it grows as the mesh is refined (1.07e-3 at 500 m to 1.69e-3 at 62 m); at a fixed ν = 10 m²/s it converges (5.7e-4 to 2.0e-4 on 10 levels, 7.9e-5 at 62 m on 20 levels). Smagorinsky `C_s` 0.5 is within 1.2–1.4× of ν = 10, `C_s` 0.2 twice that. The existing lock-exchange gates assert their mixing too (inviscid P2 8.4e-4, ν = 10 2.7e-4 W/m²).
  - `examples/farm_3d.rs` reports each 3D run's mixing (the RPE growth per area: GLS's and the advection's together). In its 3 h tidal channel `C_s` does not matter (with cages 7.3 / 6.6 / 7.3e-6 W/m² at `C_s` 0 / 0.2 / 0.5), and without cages RPE falls slightly (−1.97e-5 W/m²; the vertical advection's reversible exchange over hours, not mixing: see Fixed).

- **Advection of the GLS turbulence (TODO P4.4).** `k` and `ψ` were local to each column (as in GOTM); they are now carried by the 3D flow, as ROMS does (`gls_corstep`), so turbulence made at a tidal front, over a sill or in a farm's wake moves downstream. `Hydrostatic3D::with_turbulence_advection(Option<VerticalAdvection>)`, on by default with the tracers' limited Akima vertically; `None` keeps the columns local.
  - The mode splitter carries `Solution3D::tke`/`gls` through the 3D stages as inventories over the control volumes of the w-points (`H_w φ`, half of the bed and top layers at the ends; new `solver::rhs::w_cell_thicknesses`), like the tracers over the layers, and divides by the new thickness at the end of the step; the closure then steps production, dissipation and diffusion (operator splitting). The w-cells' fluxes are the layer transports averaged to them (new `LayerTransport::stagger_from`: half of each adjacent layer's horizontal transport, the mean of a layer's two Ω through its centre), so the averaged layer continuity is the w-cells': constancy and conservation hold as for the tracers. Upwind horizontally; inflow at open faces carries the interior's value; river water the column's own (`RiverInflow::add_w_point_sources_at_own_value`), so its volume dilutes nothing.
  - The bed and surface w-points hold the closure's boundary values (`u*²/(c_μ⁰)²` and the wall's length `κz₀`), not their half-layers' means; they are advected at their interior neighbours' values. Carried in at their own values, the surface's centimetre length cut the top interior viscosity to the background wherever Ω pointed down (`profile_3d`: the wind-driven surface current 0.10 against 0.087 m/s after one step, 0.28 against 0.175 m/s after 20).
  - Gates: a uniform `k` stays uniform under a tide over a sloping bed to 2.5e-14 (with a river too; the end w-points at 50× the interior's do not leak in), a varying `ψ` keeps its inventory to 3.4e-15 while moving by ≈ 40 %; on the wetting-and-drying beach the interior `k` stays constant to 1.7e-9 (the tracers 4.7e-9) and `ψ`'s inventory to 7.9e-11 (dry-and-rewetting nodes keep their last value, as the tracers do); with GLS k-ε, a patch of turbulence in a uniform 0.5 m/s current moves 900.01 m in 30 min (U·t = 900 m) while it decays, and stays put (1e-9 m) with the advection off. Unit gates: the w-cells' fluxes add up to the layers', `C Δσ_w ∂η/∂t` for a uniform field on closed, open and periodic meshes, zero net inventory tendency on closed and periodic ones.
  - `GlsMixing` bounds what the advection hands it: a w-point without positive `k` or `ψ` (an upwind DG undershoot) is taken as having no turbulence (`k`, `ε` at their minima), and the rest of the column is stepped as if it had been (gate `turbulence_that_is_not_positive_is_taken_as_none`). Unbounded, a negative `ψ` gave a negative or NaN `ε`, the implicit solves spread the NaN over the column, and the final bounds reset the whole column to the minima; taken as `k` with the minimum `ε` instead, its length `k^(3/2)/ε_min` mixed at ≈ 27 m²/s.
  - Cost (`examples/profile_3d.rs mixing=gls`, new `turbulence=local|advected`; 1024 P2 elements × 20 levels, 24 threads): the transport RHS 16 → 25 ms per step, the step ≈ +7 %.
  - API: `tracer_to_inventory`/`inventory_to_concentration` are renamed `to_inventory`/`from_inventory` and take the cells' σ-thicknesses (`&[f64]`) instead of a `SigmaGrid`. `ModeSplitPhysics::transport_rhs_into` also overwrites `out.tke`/`out.gls` (sized like the state's). `Solution3D`'s stage combinations (`Integrable::scale`/`axpy`) include `tke` and `gls` (an empty `other` adds nothing).

- **Net-cage drag in the 3D model (TODO F.1, 3D form).** `Hydrostatic3D::with_cage_drag(CageDrag2D)` (new module `physics::cage_drag`): the netting's `−½ C_d a |u_l| u_l` on every σ-layer the net reaches, from the surface (the net rides `η`) down to its net depth, with the layer's share inside the net (`net_fraction`: `Σ_l Δσ_l χ_l = min(d, D)/D`) and each node's footprint weight from `CageDrag2D`. Without vertical shear it is the 2D cage drag exactly.
  - Time discretisation, as the 3D bottom drag: the rates `λ_l = c_l|u_l|` are frozen at `tⁿ`; the barotropic pass applies the depth mean `−Λ̄ū`, `Λ̄ = Σ_l Δσ_l λ_l`, point-implicitly in every stage (with the bottom drag's `r/h` in one factor); `G` carries the shear part `−D Σ_l Δσ_l λ_l (u_l − ū)`; the vertical solve takes `−λ_l u_lⁿ⁺¹` on the diagonal. Thin columns feel `−Λ̄ū` with the depth mean's speed only.
  - Gate `unsheared_cage_drag_decays_like_the_2d_cage_drag`: a net deeper than the column, uniform flow decays as `u₀/(1 + c u₀ t)` to 4.8e-3, 2.4e-3, 1.2e-3 at Δt = 100, 50, 25 s (first order), uniform to 2e-16.
  - Gate `cage_drag_slows_only_the_layers_inside_the_net`: inviscid flow under a net over 5/8 of the column on four layers: each layer decays as `u₀/(1 + c χ_l u₀ t)`, `χ = (0, ½, 1, 1)`, converging at first order (3.3e-2 → 8.2e-3); without `G`'s shear part the error stalls at 7.4e-2.
  - Gate `wind_against_cages_reaches_the_column_balance`: wind against nets over the top half, no bottom drag: the nets take the whole stress, `Σ_l H_l λ_l u_l = τ/ρ₀` to 8.7e-14, the water below moves uniformly (7.8e-16); with the pass's `−Λ̄ū` alone 2.7 % off.
  - Gate `a_strong_cage_drag_is_stable_at_long_steps`: no reversal up to `c u₀ Δt = 5`; at 10–30 the explicit shear part in `G` overshoots by < 1 % of `u₀`, and nothing grows. `local_cages_give_each_column_the_2d_damping_rate`: local, overlapping cages give every column `Σ Δσ_l λ_l` equal to `CageDrag2D::damping_rate`.
  - API: `ModeSplitPhysics::layer_drag_into` (default none); `ModeSplitPhysics::vertical_implicit` and `apply_vertical_diffusion` take a `time::StepDrag { bottom, layers }` in place of the bottom-drag rates.

- **Gridded wind on the 3D model (TODO P4).** The 3D model's surface stress was one value for the whole domain (`Forcing::surface_stress`), and the gridded weather of `GriddedAtmosphere2D` could reach only the 2D module, where wind must not go in a 3D run. - **GLS turbulence closure for the 3D model (TODO P4.4).** New `physics::GlsMixing` (module `physics::gls`), a `VerticalMixing` with prognostic turbulence: the generic length scale model of Umlauf & Burchard (2003), as in ROMS (Warner et al. 2005) and GOTM. - **The mode splitter's barotropic stages run as one parallel element-block pass (TODO P4.5).** Each SSP-RK3 stage of the barotropic pass swept the 2D state serially several times after the parallel 2D RHS: `+ G`, the DU_avg2 accumulation, the stage combination (`copy_from`/`scale`/`axpy`), the depth-mean drag, and per substep the filtered average. ### Fixed

- **3D tracers left their range out of the bed and surface layers (TODO P4.5).** The vertical advection's TVD bound needs the layer beyond the upwind one; out of an end layer there is none, and `VerticalAdvection::LimitedAkima` (the default) and `Tvd` took an unbounded value between the two end layers' means. Where the surface layer sits on a plateau over a front (a light gravity current's head), sinking water left it cooler than its mean while the warm water flowing in along it carried the layer's element mean past the range: P2 lock exchanges overshot by 1.8–7.4e-5 °C with limited Akima, 2.4e-2 °C with TVD (125 m, no viscosity). The increment out of an end layer is now bounded by the end layer's own gradient, extrapolated linearly from the gradients between the three end layers' centres: linear and smoothly curved profiles keep their linear value (an internal seiche over a thick bed layer, which first order there froze, is unchanged), a plateau's water leaves at its own mean. Every configuration found is in range to 3–8e-12 °C; fronts move by ≤ 0.01 in Fr, the inviscid runs' spurious mixing by +2–3 %. Gate `vertical_values_out_of_an_end_layer_at_a_plateau_are_its_mean`.
- **Why RPE fell in the farm channel (TODO P4.5).** Not the unlimited horizontal P2 advection, as suspected: P1 and P2 fall alike and the horizontal Kuzmin limiter changes nothing. It is the low-dissipation vertical advection (upwind turns it positive), with heat and volume conserved to 5e-13. Without any tracer diffusivity the same dip, then RPE alternates with the tide and grows on balance (7.6e-5 W/m² over 12.5 h, upwind 4.5e-4): a reversible exchange of either sign over windows shorter than a tidal cycle, not a defect. `examples/farm_3d.rs` gains `limiter=none|kuzmin`, `tvadv=limited-akima|akima|tvd|upwind`, `runs=all|open` and `closure=none` (no tracer diffusivity), and reports RPE growth, the range of T and the volume and heat drift every half hour.
- **The horizontal Kuzmin tracer limiter broke the 3D rest state over a slope (TODO P4.6).** With it, the constant-`N²` seamount (`examples/seamount_3d.rs kuzmin=1`) reached 0.15 m/s within 15 min and NaN at 2 h. A σ-layer over a slope crosses the stratification, so a smooth `T(z)` varies along it, extremal over the seamount's top. The limiter bounded each node by the neighbours' means on its layer, and so clipped that variation as a front. It also scaled the layer's whole deviation from its mean by one factor, so a round-off clip at a node near the mean's height flattened the layer's stratification (growth 2× per 300 s step). `solver::limiters::tracer_3d` now:
  - bounds each node by its patch's columns at the node's own height: each column's layer means interpolated linearly in `z` to it, and the means of the column's two layers bracketing that height (the cells above and below of a 3D vertex patch, as in Thetis; Kärnä et al. 2018). Past the end layers the interpolation extrapolates, clamped to that column's nodal range there: a pit's bed node stays exact for a linear `T(z)`, and a shoreline film cannot extend its last step over metres. Bounds get a round-off margin relative to the tracer's value (256 ε). Without the bracketing means, smooth vertical extrema between thick layers (the departure of a displaced pycnocline) were cut: on the seamount at `r_x0` 0.32 the limiter changed 55–75 % of the element layers every stage, by up to 0.2 °C; with them about half as many. On a flat bed a node's own layer is one of the pair;
  - scales only the departure from the element's own column at each node's height, about its inventory-weighted mean `m`. To keep the inventory, `m` goes into each node's room within its bounds in proportion, then into the element's widest bounds, then uniformly. A factor on the column structure or a fallback to the layer mean (both tried) brought the instability back;
  - optionally limits the departure from a reference stratification: new `TracerReferenceProfile` (`new`, `from_fn`; `T(z)`, `S(z)` linear between samples) and `TracerLimiter3DConfig::with_reference_profile`. - **A stratified fluid at rest over a slope was unstable in 3D (TODO P4.6, the seamount test).** New `examples/seamount_3d.rs` (Beckmann & Haidvogel 1993: a Gaussian seamount in a doubly periodic f-plane ocean, stratified, at rest) found that even constant `N²`, which the PGF balances to round-off, did not stay at rest: the round-off velocity grew tenfold every 6 h (16 × 16 P2 elements of 4 km, 20 stretched levels, bed 40–400 m, `r_x0` 0.32; eighteenfold on 2 km elements) and blew up within 3 days, at any time step, and also at `r_x0` 0.08. Neither horizontal viscosity (10 m²/s), vertical viscosity (1e-2 m²/s), vertical diffusivity, another vertical advection scheme, a penalty on the layer velocities' face jumps nor symmetric pressure pairs stopped it.
  - Cause: the discrete PGF and the density advection did not exchange energy. In the continuum the pressure gradient's work on the flow is the potential energy the advection makes; discretely the PGF's `ρ∇z` term must be the negative adjoint of the advection of the stratification along the sloping σ-levels. The constant-depth PGF (Stelling & van Kester's vertical interpolation of the neighbour's pressure) has no such partner, nor does the conservative DG advection `∇·(Qφ)` at GLL collocation (no discrete product rule). Growth per 6 h: constant depth + conservative × 11, constant depth + split × 5, σ-pairs + conservative × 3.5, σ-pairs + split none.
  - Fix: the horizontal tracer advection is in split (flux-differencing) form within the elements, `½[∇·(Qφ) + Q·∇φ + φ∇·Q]` (new `solver::rhs::advective_divergence_element`; Kennedy & Gruber 2008, Gassner 2013): still conservative and constancy-preserving with the same layer continuity. The PGF has a new form, `PressureGradientForm::SigmaPairs` (the default): `Δp_ij = (p_j − p_i) + ½(ρ_i + ρ_j)(z_j − z_i)` along the σ-level, the σ form `∇_σp + gρ∇_σz` in flux-differencing form, whose `ρ∇z` term is under GLL summation by parts exactly the adjoint of the split-form advection; with total potential energy `Σ gρz H_z` the horizontal exchange is exact for any `ρ`. `PressureGradientForm::ConstantDepth` (the old form) stays selectable (`Hydrostatic3D::with_pressure_gradient`); `compute_pressure_gradient` takes the form.
  - The price of σ-pairs is the σ form's error at rest for curved profiles: 6.8e-4 / 1.5e-4 / 1.7e-5 m/s² on the review's pycnocline fjord at P1 500 m / P3 250 m / P3 100 m (the old element-local σ form 1.6e-3 / 1.1e-4 / 2.2e-5, constant depth 2.0e-7 / 1.5e-6 / 3.3e-6). `Hydrostatic3D::with_balanced_reference(&state)` (new `solver::rhs::BalancedReference`) takes it back: it adds the fixed field `F_z(ρ_s) − F_σ(ρ_s)` of a reference state (the initial or a parent's stratification), so a fluid resting in it feels the constant-depth force while the dynamics keep σ-pairs' energy consistency (Mellor et al. 1998's reference-profile subtraction, for any reference field). On smooth fields σ-pairs converge better than constant depth (1.73, 1.93, 1.98 against 1.69, 1.80, 1.75 at P2).
  - Seamount with a tanh pycnocline (3 kg/m³ across 10 m at 30 m, 20 levels) at `r_x0` 0.08 over 5 days: σ-pairs 1.4 → 4.1 cm/s, σ-pairs with the balanced reference 0.38 → 1.5 cm/s (growth slowing), constant depth 0.39 → 4.0 cm/s and accelerating.   - The momentum advection stays conservative: the energy argument concerns the density, and in split form the fjord-estuary gate's nodal volume budget missed 3.2e-4 instead of 3e-5 of the river.
  - Gates: `a_stratified_seamount_at_rest_stays_at_rest` (constant `N²`, 6 × 6 P2, `r_x0` 0.5, 36 h: 7.4e-11 m/s; the constant-depth form 8.6e-8 and growing, asserted as the test regime), `a_balanced_reference_gives_the_constant_depth_force_at_rest` (the whole momentum tendency to 5.9e-21 m/s² of the constant-depth form's, σ-pairs alone 2.8e-4 off). - **Balanced baroclinic pressure gradient (TODO P4.3; `REVIEW.md` §3.3).** The σ-coordinate PGF computed `∇p|_σ + gρ∇z|_σ` from an element-local derivative of a pressure integrated with a first-order half layer, and never lifted pressure jumps at element faces.   - `compute_pressure_gradient` now computes `∇p|_z` directly. Each term of the DG derivative is a pressure difference between two nodes' columns at a common depth, `Δp_ij = ½[(p_j(z_i) − p_i(z_i)) + (p_j(z_j) − p_i(z_j))]`, summed as `Σ_j Dx_ij Δp_ij` (Stelling & van Kester 1994, in a flux-differencing form).
  - Each column's `ρ − rho_ref` is a monotone Hermite cubic in z (harmonic-mean slopes, Shchepetkin & McWilliams 2003), integrated exactly from the surface.
  - Where a node lies below the other column's bed, the pair uses only the depth both columns reach.
  - The pressure jump at every element face, taken the same way, is lifted with a central flux, so a density front on a face exerts its force (and, for the full PGF, η is DG-coupled).
  - API: `compute_pressure_gradient` takes the mesh (for the face neighbours) and no longer needs `∇B`.
  - Gates (spurious |F| in m/s², fluid at rest over a 30→400 m slope):

- **Horizontal viscosity under local time stepping, parallel and allocation-free (TODO P2.5, P1.1, F.1).** `MultirateSSPRK3` now runs `with_viscosity`, and the viscous term costs a quarter of what it did.

- **Local time stepping: multirate SSP-RK3 (TODO P2.5).** On a farm-refined mesh the few smallest elements set the global time step. Now every element steps at the largest power-of-two fraction of the step that its own CFL allows.
  - `time::MultirateSSPRK3::new(max_levels)` as the `Simulation` integrator: `Simulation::new(physics, MultirateSSPRK3::new(8))`. The scheme is a multirate partitioned RK in the construction of Constantinescu & Sandu (2007), which SLIM uses (Seny et al. 2013, 2014), with SSP-RK3 as the base method. In every finest substep each element takes one SSP-RK3 step of its own over its level's Δt, with its neighbours at their stage values. The substep result is the mean over the finest substeps it spans.
    - Mass is conserved to rounding: every face flux gets the same weight on both sides.
    - SSP: the Zhang–Shu positivity bound holds per element at its own Δt, since limiters and wet/dry run on every stage value.
    - Order: SSP-RK3 within a level, second order across level interfaces. One level is SSP-RK3 bit for bit.
    - An element's stage-s RHS is evaluated only at the finest rate within s face hops, and reused in between.
  - Levels are reassigned every coarse step. An element's time step uses its own and its face neighbours' wave speeds (`solver::element_dt_swe_2d`), and neighbouring levels differ by at most one. Without these, nearly dry elements got enormous steps and water arriving from a finer neighbour made negative means (70 clips in the runup gate).
  - `SimulationResult::local_time_stepping`: `MultirateStats` (coarse steps, element RHS evaluations against global steps at the finest Δt, finest level).
  - Physics modules opt in with `PhysicsModule::local_time_stepping() -> Option<&dyn LocalTimeStepping<S>>`, and `IntegratorInfo::max_local_levels` marks the integrator. `LocalTimeStepping` has `element_dt`, a fused `stage_where` (RHS, stage combination, implicit damping, then limiting or the substep sum, all in one pass per stage) and `finish_where`. `SWEPhysics2D` implements it for every formulation, source term, boundary condition, wetting/drying, friction and cage drag. It does not support horizontal viscosity or the Kuzmin limiters, which are not element-local and panic.
  - Solver pieces: `compute_rhs_swe_2d_where_into` / `_where_then` (the RHS of a subset of elements, each at its own time, bit for bit the full RHS on them), `ImplicitDamping2D::damp_element`, `apply_wet_dry_correction_element`, `StandardLimiter2D::apply_element`.
  - Gates (`tests/local_time_stepping_test.rs`):
    - One level is SSP-RK3 bit for bit, with WetDry, positivity, wet/dry, implicit friction and a time-dependent source.
    - The subset RHS is bit for bit the full one.
    - Mass is conserved across four levels (1.7e-14 relative drift, against 4.6e-14 for global SSP-RK3).
    - A lake at rest over a rough bed with a dry island holds to 1e-10.
    - A uniform y-force on an x-graded mesh is integrated to 1e-6: stage times per element are right.
    - Temporal convergence to the global solution at orders 2.6–2.8.
    - A runup across levels has no clip and h ≥ 0.
    - The farm mesh saves 2.6× of the RHS work.
  - **Accuracy cost:** the interface coupling dominates the time error, ≈ 50× that of global SSP-RK3 at the same CFL for a gravity wave crossing four levels (1.5e-3 relative at CFL 0.8). It scales as (λΔt)², so it is negligible for tides.
  - **Farm benchmark** (`examples/local_time_stepping_farm.rs`, P2, 0.5 h of M2 from rest with cages, friction and Coriolis): 3,584 quads from 12 to 455 m (`tests/data/gmsh/fjord_farm.msh`, from `scripts/gmsh_farm_mesh.py`).

- **Drag of fish-farm net cages, 2D form (TODO F.1).** A net cage is a porous momentum sink, S = −½ C_d a |u| u per unit volume, integrated over the net depth: ∂(hu)/∂t = −½ C_d a min(d, h) |u| u.

- **Second-order subcell finite volumes for `SWEFormulation2D::WetDry` (TODO P1.2).**

### Performance

- **Coastline meshes without degenerate quads: the Frøya coastline run is 1.84× faster (TODO P1.3).** The smallest time step of the Frøya coastline mesh (0.0096 s) came from nearly degenerate quads, not small ones. Gmsh's default minimum recombination quality (0.01) pairs triangles along straight coastline into quads with two coastline edges, and after subdivision these have corners of up to 178°, where the bilinear Jacobian nearly vanishes. `scripts/gmsh_coastline_mesh.py` now recombines only pairs of quality ≥ 0.3 (`recombine_quality=`) and smooths 5 passes (`smooth=`): 2 % more quads, the largest corner 153° instead of 178°, and the smallest step 0.112 s (11.6×). `froya_real_data mesh=… lts=8`, 30 model minutes at 24 threads: 108 → 59 s, with 5 instead of 8 time-step levels. The script prints the largest corner angle, and `docs/gmsh-meshes.md` recommends `Mesh.RecombineMinimumQuality = 0.3`. Regenerate `data/froya_coast.msh` to get it.

- **Cheaper wet/dry stage work on the Frøya case (TODO P2.2, P2.3).** Measured with the new `froya_real_data profile=N` phase timer, single thread; on battery power, so indicative only.
  - Point-implicit Manning damping: 3.5 → 1.3 ms per call. `hypot` became `sqrt(x² + y²)` (MSVC's `hypot` is a C runtime call), and a Halley `cbrt` (≤ 2 ulp of `f64::cbrt`, 2× faster than the CRT's) replaced the CRT `cbrt`.
  - Post-processing: 0.84 → 0.41 ms per call.
    - The positivity limiter takes each element mean inside its element kernel, with no separate pass or allocation.
    - `SWEPhysics2D::post_process` skips a `Positivity(h ≤ h_dry)` limiter when wet/dry is on: the wet/dry correction applies the same limiter to every element it would change, so the result is bitwise identical.
    - `LimiterContext2D` no longer computes the unused `h_min`, a serial sqrt per edge every stage.
  - The WetDry hydrostatic HLL passes the node velocities to a face-aligned HLL core (`hll_flux_face_aligned`) instead of rebuilding momenta and dividing them back out. `hll_flux_swe_2d` is bitwise unchanged.
  - Profile findings (TODO P2): HLL is about a third of the single-thread time (every face is solved twice), and scaling stops at about 8 threads on the test laptop (4 Zen 5 + 8 Zen 5c cores).
- **One numerical flux per interior face for the split forms (TODO P2.2).**
  - A face pass (`SplitFormSWE2D::edge_fluxes`, parallel over edges) evaluates F* once for both sides into a thread-cached buffer, and the element loop reads it.

  - The RHS equals the old one to round-off (≤ 4e-19 on the snapshot cases).
  - Frøya, single thread, pinned, on mains power: RHS 5.3 → 4.85 ms. The face-aligned HLL core had already taken it from ≈ 5.9 ms. Neutral at 24 threads.
  - `SWEState2D` implements `Neg`.

### Fixed

- **A sloping bed without wet/dry pushed nothing by default (TODO P1.2).** `SWEPhysics2DBuilder` without wetting/drying defaulted to `Standard`, whose bed-slope force comes only from an explicit `BathymetrySource2D`. `with_bathymetry` alone over a bed that is not flat therefore built a model in which the bed did nothing: a lake at rest in a 30 km channel over a 20 → 10 m slope piled up to η = +10 m at the shallow end within an hour.
  - The default is now `EntropyStable` for a bed that is not flat, unless a source carries the slope (`BathymetrySource2D` keeps `Standard`). An explicit `Standard` over such a bed without that source panics at `build`, naming the fix. Flat beds and wet/dry runs are unchanged.
  - Regression test `a_sloping_bed_is_balanced_by_default`: the channel stays at rest to 1e-10 m. Unit tests for the other defaults and the panic.
  - `several_levels_conserve_mass` (local time stepping) had relied on the trap: the spurious flow gave it a third level. With the slope applied (`EntropyStable`, or `Standard` with the source) it has two, and the test now asks for two.

- **`WetDry` subcells let a dry node below the water flood (TODO P3.1).** The 15-day Frøya run had 4.7–4.9 m/s at spring tides at (−16.3, −14.2) km. There, one element holds a one-node-wide wet strip (bed −1.4 to −5.5 m) between land at +4 m and a sill node at +0.35 m. On every strong flood the sill stayed dry under 0.45–0.65 m of water. Meanwhile the strip piled up 0.15–0.3 m above its surroundings, and a 3–5 m/s jet built up pushing at the sill. When the sill finally wetted, everything equalised within the hour.
  - Cause: the subcell reconstruction took a dry node's η = B for a water level. At the strip node the η stencil (bank 4.0, strip 0.8, sill 0.35) is monotone. The limited slope then set the face value toward the sill to exactly the sill's bed, so the hydrostatic reconstruction gave h* = 0 there, however high the strip rose. The same slope tilted the subcell's face beds, and its bed term pushed the water against the closed face. A dry node between a higher and a lower wet node had the same problem: its face bed was lifted to the higher surface.
  - Fix (`reconstruct_subcell`): η is not reconstructed (zero slope) in a stencil with a dry node (h < `h_dry`) below one of its wet surfaces. Banks above the water keep the second-order reconstruction, and at lake at rest every dry node is such a bank, so balance is unchanged.
  - Frøya, hours 0–25 from 2025-05-31: the sill wets on every flood as soon as the water passes 0.35 m. At hour 24, 4.73 m/s in the strip (η 0.80 against 0.62 m beyond the sill) becomes 0.03 m/s (η 0.62 on both sides). The domain's largest speed is now the known (10.6, −13.8) km pinnacle (2.83 m/s). Volume and cost are unchanged.
  - Accuracy: Thacker WetDry P2 5.7 % / 1.75 % at 16² / 32² (was 5.5 % / 1.7 %). Zeroing the slope next to every dry node instead cost 2.5 % at 32².
  - API: `SWE2DRhsConfig::with_subcell_depth` (field `subcell_depth`) runs the subcells in every element with a node shallower than a given depth, without calling those nodes dry. The surface term is now `F(q⁻)·n − F*(q*⁻, q*⁺) + ½g(h*⁻² − h⁻²)·n`: nodal-state interior flux, numerical flux on the reconstructed states, and the Audusse momentum correction (new `HydrostaticReconstruction::pressure_correction`, pressure taken as zero for `h ≤ h_min`), the 1D analogue of the 2D `HydrostaticReconstruction2D::pressure_correction` fix. On a periodic 20 km domain with cell-averaged B = −200 + 150·sin(2πx/L) and a slope-correlated flow, the mass rate went from −1.1e-4 of the total mass per second to ~1e-20 (p = 1–4; Roe, HLL and Lax–Friedrichs). Lake-at-rest over the cell-averaged bed stays at round-off. Regression tests: `test_conservation_discontinuous_state_flat_bottom`, `test_mass_conservation_hydrostatic_reconstruction_discontinuous_bathymetry`, `test_lake_at_rest_cell_averaged_bathymetry`, `test_pressure_correction_balances_lake_at_rest`, and new manufactured-solution convergence tests `test_convergence_swe_1d_p{1,2,3}` (observed orders 2.1 / 3.0 / 4.0 for all three fluxes; all went to NaN before the fix).
